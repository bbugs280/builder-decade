Ask someone what it costs to build a product solo and they'll name the visible line items: a coding-tool subscription, an API bill, a domain, maybe a designer. Those are the cheap parts, and they total to less than you think.

The expensive parts never show up on an invoice. They're time, attention, and the quiet load of being the only decision-maker. This is the honest accounting no one gives you before you start — and it matters, because the thing that actually sinks solo projects isn't running out of money. It's misreading what the real cost is.

## The visible costs (so we can put them in perspective)

To be clear upfront: the cash cost has genuinely collapsed, and that's real. A solo builder in 2026 can ship a working product for well under a hundred dollars a month — a coding assistant, a model, hosting, a domain. That's the story the other posts tell, and it's true.

But here's the trap hiding inside it: **because the money cost is now almost nothing, it's easy to conclude the cost is nothing.** That's wrong. The cost just moved, from *cash* to *time and attention*, and those are the two things you can never buy back.

## The real cost #1: time is the fixed budget

Cash is flexible — you can earn more, spend less, raise it, defer it. Time is not. Every hour you put into the product is an hour not spent on your job, your family, your health, or sleeping.

Solo building doesn't add hours to your week; it *takes* them. And because there's no co-founder to trade shifts with, when you stop, the product stops. There's no one to carry it. That single fact — that the project only moves when *you* move it — is the most expensive hidden cost in the entire model.

## The real cost #2: attention is the scarce resource

Money you can budget. Attention you can't. The hardest part of building solo isn't the building — it's the *context-switching*. You're the engineer, the designer, the marketer, the support desk, and the CEO, and you have to be all of them on the same day, sometimes in the same hour.

Every switch costs something. The tax of holding a dozen roles in one head is not visible in any time tracker, but it's the thing that turns a three-hour build session into a six-hour crawl. Solo doesn't mean "one job." It means *all* the jobs, serialized through one brain.

## The real cost #3: the decision load

This is the quietest and heaviest of the three. With a team, decisions get distributed — someone owns design, someone owns infra, someone owns the roadmap. Solo, *every* decision is yours, and there are thousands more than you expect.

What to name it. Which feature to cut. Whether that bug matters. Whether this is good enough to ship or needs one more pass. None of these are hard individually. The cost is the *volume* — the constant, low-grade decision fatigue that erodes your judgment exactly when you need it most. This is why so many solo builders stall not at the hard part, but at the hundredth easy part.

## So what do you do about it?

The honest answer isn't "spend more money" — it's these three moves, each aimed at the real cost:

**1. Shrink the project until time stops being the enemy.** The smaller the win condition, the fewer hours it takes to reach it, the less the fixed cost of time hurts. This is why "ship one tiny thing" is a survival strategy, not a slogan.

**2. Kill the context-switching.** Batch your roles: a day for building, a day for marketing, a morning for support — instead of all of them every day. The tax isn't the work; it's the *switching*.

**3. Reduce decisions by writing them down.** A single doc with your defaults — the name, the scope, the "good enough" bar — means you decide once and reuse a hundred times. The goal isn't better decisions, it's *fewer*.

## The honest bottom line

The money cost of shipping solo has collapsed to almost nothing. That part is real, and it's why the one-person wave exists.

But the cost didn't disappear — it moved into time, attention, and decision load, the three resources you can't outsource and can't buy back. Build solo, but build with your eyes open about which one of those is actually rationing you.
