A year ago this site was an empty scaffold and a promise. A year of building later, there's a body of work: things shipped, things broken, things learned. This is the honest ledger of that year — what actually compounded, what quietly didn't, and the one insight I'd reread on day one if I could.

## What actually compounded

**The streak, not the sprint.** The single biggest lesson, and the least glamorous. Consistent small sessions beat heroic weekends every time, because the streak keeps the context warm and the weekends keep *relearning* it. I'd heard this for years. I didn't believe it until I felt the difference.

**Shipped things, not plans.** Every shipped thing — however small — became evidence. Evidence that I could ship, evidence others could see, evidence that compounded into confidence. The plans I didn't ship compounded into nothing. This asymmetry is the whole game.

**The logs.** Writing down one line per build — what worked, what broke, what I'd do differently — turned a year of work into a personal playbook. A year of un-logged work would have been a year of *forgetting*.

**The tools I stopped swapping.** The biggest productivity gain wasn't finding a better tool. It was stopping the search, committing to one stack, and letting familiarity compound.

## What quietly didn't

**The calendar of "someday."** Every "I'll do that when things settle down" item is still there, untouched. Someday doesn't compound. Only shipped things do.

**The polish I never scheduled.** The parts that felt "good enough" stayed "good enough" for a year — and "good enough" built up a debt. Taste, it turns out, is a deliverable that has to be scheduled, or it doesn't happen.

**The audience I never built.** I shipped into a vacuum for months, assuming the work would eventually speak for itself. It doesn't. Showing one person — then a few — is itself a discipline, and I started it later than I should have.

**The "one big launch."** The fantasy of a single blowout that finally puts everything on the map doesn't survive contact with a year of reality. What moved the needle was always the unglamorous, weekly, low-stakes shipping — never the launch.

## The insight I'd reread on day one

If I could hand my past self one sentence, it would be this:

**"The work compounds, but only when it ships — and only the small, consistent, shipped things compound at all."**

Not the ideas. Not the plans. Not the setup. The shipped things. One per week, slightly embarrassing, compounding into a body of work that eventually looks like talent. That's the entire secret, and it's not a secret at all — it's just hard to sit still and do, which is why most people don't.

## Where year two points

The year established the loop. Year two is about the same loop, aimed better: fewer things, chosen with more judgment, shipped with more consistency, shown to more people. The tools will keep collapsing costs. The judgment will keep being the hard part. And the streak will keep being the engine.

A decade is just ten of these years, stacked. The first one taught me the loop. The next nine are about running it.
