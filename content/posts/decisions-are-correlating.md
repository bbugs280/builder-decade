A brokerage just gave every customer a button that trades for them. Over 150,000 people turned it on in four months. The agents hit the platform's tools about 30 million times a day.

That's not a bug report. It's a structural change, and it matters more than the headlines about "AI trading" suggest.

Here's the thing nobody says plainly: those 150,000 accounts aren't running 150,000 independent strategies. They're choosing from a *handful* of shared models — the same few AI systems, pointed at the same market data, optimizing toward roughly the same goals.

A market works because the people feeding it *disagree*. Millions of small, independent decisions — wrong, right, late, early — average out into a price you can mostly trust. Strip out the independence and you don't have a market of individuals anymore. You have **one decision, copied a few hundred thousand times**.

That's the shift to watch, and it's the opposite of the usual fear. The worry isn't that the AI is irrational. It's that the AI is *identical*.

## Correlation is the word to watch for

The people who run markets have already started saying it. Regulators asked, in writing, what happens when "large numbers of participants rely on similar models" and produce "correlated trading decisions." A central banker put it bluntly: human crowds panic at *different speeds* — some hesitate, some bail early, some hold. A population of identical agents doesn't. They all respond to the same signal the same way, at the same moment. One wobble becomes a rout before anyone can slow it down.

That's not speculative scaremongering. It's the *mechanism* — and it's the first time the mechanism has been named this clearly by the institutions that would have to clean up the mess.

## The honest counter — and why it matters

Here's the part that keeps this from being a doom post. The strongest evidence in the *other* direction came years ago, and it still holds: when researchers looked at algorithmic trading across an entire currency market, they found the algorithms *were* correlated — but that correlation had **no measurable link to higher volatility**. If anything, more algorithmic trading tracked with *lower* volatility.

So the truthful position is narrower and more interesting than "AI crashes markets." It's this: **correlation is real, and whether it damps or amplifies volatility depends entirely on what the shared models are optimizing toward — and right now, nobody knows.**

The risk isn't decided. The risk is *unmeasured*.

## What this means for the solo builder

Most advice about differentiation is about standing out — the moat, the positioning, the "be different so you win." This reframes it. When everyone ships on the same models, the sameness stops being a *marketing* problem and becomes a *safety* problem — for you, and for whatever you're part of.

Your leverage was never going to be the model. It's the things you own that the shared model doesn't: your data, your rules, your specific problem, your taste. That's what [harness-not-model](/posts/harness-not-model/) means by "you rent the brain, you build the rig." But there's a second, less obvious reason to own your context.

**Owned context is what makes your output not-a-copy.** When you train on *your* users, *your* failures, *your* niche's edge cases, you produce decisions that are genuinely your own. They won't correlate with the other builders on your street the way the out-of-the-box answers do. You stop being one more echo of the same model.

In a world where sameness is becoming a systemic risk, deliberate difference isn't a vanity metric. It's a form of resilience — yours, and the system's.

## The one-line version

If a model is cheap and everyone has it, the model isn't a strategy — it's a backdrop. What you actually own is the context you feed it, and the rules you set. That's been the argument for a harness for a while now. The new part is *why it's urgent*: because when everyone ships the same AI, the sameness is what breaks things — not the intelligence.
